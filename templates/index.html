About the Project

Crop Maturity Detector

A final-year machine learning project that uses deep learning to automate crop ripeness classification — reducing post-harvest losses and helping farmers make data-driven decisions.

What We Built

The Crop Maturity Detector is an AI-powered web application that classifies crops into three maturity stages — Unripe, Ripe, and Overripe — from a single photograph.

Using MobileNetV2 as the backbone with transfer learning from ImageNet weights, we fine-tuned the model on a curated dataset of crop images. The model uses 96×96 RGB inputs and returns class probabilities; inference speed depends on the device.

The result is a lightweight, accurate, and accessible tool that can be used directly from a smartphone browser — no app installation required.

Problem Statement
Post-harvest losses account for 30–40% of food production in developing countries. Inaccurate harvesting decisions — too early or too late — cause significant economic and nutritional loss.
💡
Our Solution
A MobileNetV2 transfer-learning classifier for three maturity labels (Unripe, Ripen, Overripe). Evaluation metrics are loaded from Kaggle results after training.
🔬
Methodology
Images resized to 96×96 px with ImageNet channel normalization. Training uses augmentation, an AdamW optimizer, and class-weighted cross-entropy loss.
📈
Impact
A lightweight model suitable for local inference. Real-world speed and accuracy depend on the device, image quality, and crop variety; the result should support—not replace—field inspection.
🚀
Future Scope
Expanding to multi-crop detection, IoT sensor integration for field deployment, mobile app, and real-time drone-based crop monitoring systems.

Understanding Dataset Class Imbalance & Macro Metrics

A critical scientific takeaway from this evaluation is the substantial class imbalance in the Fruits-360 test split and its direct impact on aggregate performance metrics:

1. Extreme Distribution Skew Of the 43,581 official test images, 40,049 (91.89%) belong to the Ripen class, while only 1,671 (3.83%) are Unripe and 1,861 (4.27%) are Overripe.
2. Why Overall Accuracy is 93.07% Overall accuracy is sample-weighted: each correct prediction counts equally. Because 91.89% of the dataset is in the Ripen class and the model classifies 37,170 of them correctly, the majority class dominates and elevates overall accuracy to 93.07%.
3. Why Macro Precision (69.83%) & F1 (78.36%) are Lower Macro metrics calculate the unweighted arithmetic mean across classes (33.3% weight each). While 1,863 Ripen fruits misclassified as Unripe is a small fraction of the Ripen pool (4.6%), it represents more than the entire true Unripe test set (1,671). This drops Unripe Precision to 45.73% (1,576 / 3,446), pulling Macro Precision to 69.83% and Macro F1 to 78.36%.

⚠️ Agronomic Importance of Macro Metrics

In agriculture, predicting an unripe crop as ripe or vice versa can result in premature harvesting or spoilage. Tracking unweighted macro metrics ensures model monitoring is not blinded by a disproportionately large majority class.

Evaluation Scope & Real Farm Photography Limitations

While the MobileNetV2 checkpoint achieves strong performance on the benchmark dataset, rigorous scientific transparency requires acknowledging the boundaries of laboratory testing:

🔬 Fruits-360 Controlled Test Environment

The Fruits-360 dataset consists of single fruits mounted on a slowly rotating motor against a uniform white backdrop with high-key studio lighting. There are no shadows from trees, overlapping fruits, or background foliage.

🚜 Real Farm & Orchard Conditions

This evaluation does not establish accuracy on real farm or smartphone photographs. Sunlight angle, canopy shade, dirt/dust on peels, motion blur, and variable optics introduce significant distribution shifts.

🛡️ How HarvestAI Safeguards Decisions

Because studio accuracy does not guarantee field accuracy, HarvestAI uses a two-stage pipeline: an SSDLite320 detector verifies supported produce before classification, and any prediction below 70% confidence is flagged as "Uncertain Prediction — Please Verify", accompanied by crop-specific tactile and physical verification guidance.

📌 Distinction Between Historical Experiments and Production Model

In the development notebook, HOG + SVM used a smaller stratified training subset and was subsequently evaluated on the Fruits-360 official test set, while the Custom CNN architecture had separate official test evaluations in earlier notebook experiments. MobileNetV2 (Epoch 5) represents the currently deployed checkpoint with 93.07% test accuracy and 78.36% macro F1. Because these models originated from different training runs, dataset subsets, and experimental protocols, their outcomes should be clearly identified by protocol rather than presented as a fully controlled comparison. MobileNetV2 was selected for deployment due to its parameter-efficient classifier transfer (3,843 trainable parameters), lightweight edge inference latency, and verified evaluation on the official test set.

Research results measure dataset performance on the Fruits-360 official test split, not guaranteed accuracy on smartphone or farm photographs. Fruits-360 contains controlled-background images and maturity metadata attached to varieties.

Agronomy Knowledge

Farmer Tips

General educational tips only. Verify harvest decisions with crop-specific guidance and field inspection.

🌡️

Harvest Early Morning

Ambient field temperatures are lowest at dawn. Harvesting before sunrise cuts post-harvest respiration and boosts shelf life by up to 40%.

💧

The Weight Test

Heavier produce correlates with higher juice density and dissolved sugars. Light fruits usually signal dry pulp or hollow cavities.

🌙

Moon Cycles

Traditional and biodynamic farming schedules favor harvesting root vegetables during the new moon, and above-ground juicy fruits near full moon.

📦

Post-Harvest Cooling

Pre-cool harvested produce within 2 hours. Every hour produce sits uncooled at 30°C can cost an entire day of fresh market life.

👍

The Thumb Test

Gentle tactile pressure reveals cell wall pectin: a slight give indicates peak ripeness, no give is unripe, and sinking fingers indicate overripeness.

🌿

Ethylene Awareness

Apples and bananas emit potent ethylene gas. Keep them isolated from greens, berries, and melons unless you intend to trigger rapid ripening.
